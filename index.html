Simulator settings Without an Arduino, a simulated bladder answers the same commands as the real one. It fills at 0.8 psi/s up to 3 psi and bends the finger 30° per psi, and its loss is L = L₀ + k (θ − θ₀). With the Arduino connected, the angle comes from the calibration fit in src/main.cpp instead. Capture points Fit the bend angle to the optical loss, then paste the result into the sketch. 1. Connect with the bladder empty. The optical loss is measured against the fixed WAVEGUIDE_BASELINE in src/main.cpp, so don't change the LED, potentiometer or wiring between capturing and using the fit. 2. Capture 0° first. Then bend the finger to angles across the range you'll use, by hand or with Inflate and Hold. 3. At each bend, measure the angle with a protractor, type it in and press Capture. Hold still while it averages. 4. 6 to 10 points spread over the range is plenty. Capturing 0° again at the end shows whether the reading drifted. 5. Copy the coefficients into src/main.cpp and upload.

    Light sensor on A1 
            The waveguide's photodiode, read with analogRead(A1): 0 to 1023 counts for 0 to 5 V. More light gives a higher reading, so bending the finger lowers it.
            Baseline is the fixed reference WAVEGUIDE_BASELINE in src/main.cpp, the straight-finger reading when the angle was calibrated. The optical loss compares every reading with it. With the finger straight the reading should be close to 100%; if not, the light level has changed and the angles are off.
            0 counts: no light reaches the photodiode, or it isn't wired to A1. Check that the LED is lit, the potentiometer isn't turned all the way down (0 Ω gives 0 V) and the photodiode's signal wire goes to A1.
            1023 counts: the reading is at 5 V and can't show small changes. Turn the potentiometer down.
            The shaded part of the bar, 3 to 4 V, is a good place for the straight-finger reading: fine steps, with room left below 5 V.
