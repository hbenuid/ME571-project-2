Bend your finger to fly up and straighten it to fly down. Dodge the asteroids.

The bladder stays vented, so the finger moves under its own effort. Space also starts the game. Without an Arduino, hold ↑ and ↓ to move the simulated finger.

Without an Arduino, a simulated bladder answers the same commands as the real one. It fills at 0.8 psi/s up to 3 psi and bends the finger 30° per psi, and its loss is L = L₀ + k (θ − θ₀). With the Arduino connected, the angle comes from the calibration fit in src/main.cpp instead. Make that fit on the Calibration page.

Chrome, Edge or Firefox 151+, not Safari. Close the PlatformIO serial monitor first, since only one program can open the port. Connecting usually restarts the sketch, which vents for 5 s and re-zeros the optical and pressure baselines, so keep the finger straight. If this page stops responding, the Arduino vents within 1.5 s and hands control back to the FSR.

Records everything the Arduino sends, from Connect to Disconnect. The trial name goes in the file name and in every row.

  1. Connect with the finger straight and the bladder empty. The sketch zeros the optical loss as it starts.
  2. Capture 0° first. Then bend the finger to angles across the range you'll use, by hand or with Inflate and Hold.
  3. At each bend, measure the angle with a protractor, type it in and press Capture. Hold still while it averages.
  4. 6 to 10 points spread over the range is plenty. Capturing 0° again at the end shows whether the reading drifted.
  5. Copy the coefficients into src/main.cpp and upload.
